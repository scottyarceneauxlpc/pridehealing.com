LGBTQIA+ Care

Affirming counseling for the LGBTQIA+ community in Louisiana

Your identity is not the problem to be solved. It is part of who you are, and it is welcome in this space exactly as it is. Care here is delivered by secure telehealth across the Acadiana region and throughout Louisiana, which means you can find an affirming counselor without needing one in your own town. In-person visits are also coming soon.

What Affirming Care Means Here

Therapy that starts by believing you

Too many LGBTQIA+ people have sat across from a counselor and spent the hour explaining or defending themselves instead of getting help. Some have been told their identity was a symptom. Some have been pushed toward changing it. That is not counseling, and it is not what happens here.

In practical terms, affirming care means your identity is treated as a given rather than a question. Sessions focus on what you actually came in for, whether that is anxiety, depression, trauma, recovery, grief, or the ordinary hard parts of being a person.

  • Your name and pronouns are used correctly, from the first contact onward
  • Your identity is never treated as a disorder or a cause of your symptoms
  • No practice that attempts to change sexual orientation or gender identity is used here, ever
  • You decide how much of your history you want to discuss, and when
  • Stress from discrimination and rejection is understood as an injury done to you, not a flaw in you

Common Reasons People Reach Out

What we can work on together

Some people come in because of something tied directly to identity. Others come in for reasons that have nothing to do with it. Both are entirely valid reasons to start.

Minority stress and burnout

The cumulative weight of vigilance, misgendering, microaggressions, and bracing for the next hostile comment. This kind of chronic stress has real effects on mood, sleep, and health, and it responds to treatment.

Anxiety and depression

Often the reason people call, and often intertwined with living somewhere that does not feel safe to be fully known. We work on symptoms and circumstances at the same time.

Coming out, or not

Whether, when, how, and to whom is your decision alone. Therapy can be a place to think it through out loud without anyone pushing an agenda, including staying private if that is the safer choice right now.

Family strain and rejection

Being unsupported by the people who were supposed to be your foundation is a specific kind of grief. We work on boundaries, expectations, and grieving the relationship you needed but did not get.

Religious and spiritual harm

Being taught that who you are is broken leaves a mark, particularly across South Louisiana where faith and family are deeply woven together. Untangling belief from shame is difficult, and it is possible.

Gender identity exploration

A space to think about gender without a required destination or timeline. Questioning is allowed. Uncertainty is allowed. Changing your mind is allowed.

Substance use and recovery

LGBTQIA+ people face higher rates of substance use, frequently as a way of coping with stress and isolation. As a Licensed Addiction Counselor, that work happens here alongside the identity-related context driving it.

Relationships and intimacy

Communication, conflict, jealousy, monogamy and non-monogamy, sexual concerns, and the particular strain of navigating a relationship that others may not accept.

Trauma and PTSD

Including harassment, assault, bullying, family violence, and the accumulated harm of being unsafe over a long stretch of time.

Grief and loss

For people, for relationships, and sometimes for the years and the version of yourself lost to hiding.

Practical Details

The small things that make a space safe

Affirming care is not only about the conversation. It is also about the paperwork, the records, and the logistics that often go wrong elsewhere.

  • Intake collects your name and pronouns, and your chosen name is what gets used in session
  • If your legal name differs from your name, insurance may require the legal one on claims — we will discuss that openly before anything is filed
  • Telehealth means no waiting room, and no risk of running into someone you know locally
  • Sessions from home need only a private space and a stable connection, across the Acadiana region and anywhere in Louisiana
  • Records are protected under HIPAA, and what you share is confidential within the legal limits explained at intake
  • If you would rather pay privately instead of using insurance to keep your care off an insurance record, that option is available

For Parents and Family Members

If someone you love just came out to you

You may be somewhere between wanting to support them and not knowing how. You may be carrying beliefs you were raised with that suddenly sit uncomfortably against your love for this person. That tension is worth working through, and it is worth working through somewhere other than at them.

Research on family acceptance is unusually clear: family support is strongly protective for LGBTQIA+ people's mental health, and family rejection is strongly associated with harm. Your response genuinely matters. Counseling can be a place to ask the questions you do not want to burden them with, and to figure out how to show up well.

If You Need Support Right Now

Crisis lines that specialize in LGBTQIA+ callers

These are not affiliated with this practice. They are staffed around the clock or on posted hours, and they are free.

988988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

Call or text 988 for free, confidential crisis support at any hour. Please note: 988's dedicated LGBTQ+ option, once reached by pressing 3, was discontinued in July 2025 and has not been restored as of August 2026. You will still reach a trained crisis counselor.

Call or text 988 · 24/7

TPThe Trevor Project

Crisis intervention and suicide prevention built specifically for LGBTQ+ young people under 25, by phone, text, or chat.

1-866-488-7386 · text START to 678678 · 24/7

thetrevorproject.org

TLiTrans Lifeline

A peer support line run by trans people, for trans and questioning people. You do not need to be in crisis to call. Note that this line runs on limited weekday hours rather than around the clock.

1-877-565-8860 · Mon–Fri, about noon–8 p.m. Central

translifeline.org

LNLGBT National Help Center

Free, confidential peer support staffed by LGBTQIA+ volunteers, with separate lines for youth, seniors, and people working through coming out.

Hotline 1-888-843-4564 · Youth 1-800-246-7743

Coming out support 1-888-688-5428

lgbthotline.org

CBCall BlackLine

Peer support and counseling centered on a Black, Black LGBTQ+, and Black femme lens, prioritizing Black, Brown, Indigenous, and Muslim callers.

Call or text 1-800-604-5841

callblackline.com

CTCrisis Text Line

Free, confidential text-based crisis support with a trained volunteer counselor. Not LGBTQIA+ specific, but available at any hour.

Text HOME to 741741 · 24/7

crisistextline.org

Community Near You

Louisiana and Acadiana organizations

Counseling is one kind of support. Community is another, and the two work best together. There is no LGBTQIA+ organization based in New Iberia or Iberia Parish, but Lafayette is close, and several groups serve the whole state.

PLPride Lafayette

Acadiana's LGBTQ+ community center, at 640 Main Street in downtown Lafayette. Home base for local support groups and social programming. Center hours depend on the hosting schedule, so check the community calendar before going.

pridelafayette.org

TLaTrans Lafayette

A support group for transgender and gender-diverse people, people with intersex traits or differences of sexual development, and allies. Meets the first and third Sunday of each month, 4:30–6:00 p.m., at the Pride Lafayette center.

pridelafayette.org/trans

LTLouisiana Trans Advocates

Statewide trans-led advocacy and social support, with in-person support meetings in Lafayette, Baton Rouge, New Orleans, Lake Charles, and Shreveport, plus an online group for people who cannot travel. Also publishes Louisiana-specific guidance on updating name and gender markers on identity documents.

latransadvocates.org

AQAcadiana Queer Collective

Builds safe, accepting community space for LGBTQIA+ people of all ages across Acadiana, through Pride Acadiana, family meetups, a book club, an inclusive prom, and other year-round programming.

acadianaqueercollective.org

FEForum for Equality

Louisiana's statewide LGBTQ+ civil rights advocacy organization, working on policy and legal protections at the state level.

forumforequality.org

PFPFLAG

Support, education, and community for LGBTQIA+ people together with their parents, families, and allies. Louisiana chapters include New Orleans and Baton Rouge; use the chapter finder for currently active groups near you.

pflag.org/findachapter

Further Support

Legal help, health care, and national organizations

ATAdvocates for Trans Equality

Formed by the merger of the National Center for Transgender Equality and the Transgender Legal Defense & Education Fund. Policy advocacy plus practical guides on identity documents and health care access.

transequality.org

LLLambda Legal

Legal advocacy for LGBTQ+ people and people living with HIV, including a help desk for people facing discrimination.

lambdalegal.org

TLCTransgender Law Center

Legal support and advocacy focused on the rights of transgender and gender-nonconforming people, including health care access and employment.

transgenderlawcenter.org

PPPoint of Pride

Direct practical assistance for trans people, including free chest binders, hair removal and gender-affirming surgery funds, and HRT access support.

pointofpride.org

LHLGBTQ+ Healthcare Directory

A free, searchable directory of health care providers who have identified themselves as affirming, useful for finding a primary care doctor or specialist.

lgbtqhealthcaredirectory.org

FAFamily Acceptance Project

Research-based education and resources for families working to support their LGBTQ+ children, including materials written for religious families.

familyproject.sfsu.edu

LNMLGBT Near Me

A searchable national directory of LGBTQ+ community centers, support groups, and services — a good way to find affirming spaces close to home or when traveling.

lgbtnearme.org

SBStopBullying.gov

Federal guidance on preventing and responding to bullying and cyberbullying, with age-appropriate resources for LGBTQ+ youth, parents, and educators.

stopbullying.gov

Ready When You Are

You are welcome here

If you have been putting off finding a counselor because you were not sure whether you would be safe, that is a reasonable thing to have worried about. You can request an appointment online in a few minutes, or send a secure message first if you would rather ask a question before booking.

Begin Your Healing Journey

Questions first? Send a secure message or call (337) 283-9997. For a broader list of local support, see the community resources page.

New here? Requests are typically answered within 24 hours — evenings and weekends included.
