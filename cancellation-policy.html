Cancellation & No-Show Policy

Life happens — here's how missed sessions are handled

Your appointment time is held exclusively for you, and when a session is missed without notice, that hour can't be offered to someone else who needed it. This policy exists to keep scheduling fair and predictable — not to punish anyone for having a hard week.

Last updated: August 2026 · This policy is reviewed and signed during intake.

The Short Version

Three things to remember

Situation What Happens
Cancel or reschedule with 24+ hours' noticeBy phone, email, or the client portal No charge
Cancel with less than 24 hours' noticeOr arriving more than 10 minutes late First time: no charge
After that: full session fee
No-show — no contact at allSession time passes without you joining First time: no charge
After that: full session fee

Everyone gets one grace occurrence. Genuine emergencies and sudden illness are waived beyond that as well — just reach out and say so.

Giving Notice

24 hours, by whatever method is easiest

To cancel or reschedule without a fee, please give at least 24 hours' notice before your scheduled start time. Notice can be given any of these ways, and any one of them counts:

  • Call or text (337) 283-9997 — a voicemail left before the 24-hour mark counts as notice
  • Email admin@pridehealing.com
  • Cancel or reschedule yourself through the client portal at pridehealing.clientsecure.me

You never need to explain why. "I need to reschedule" is a complete sentence, and it will never be met with pushback or follow-up questions.

Late Arrivals

The 10-minute mark

If you haven't joined the session within 10 minutes of the scheduled start time and there's been no word from you, the appointment is treated as a no-show and the time is released. If you do join late within that window, the session still ends at its original scheduled time so the next client isn't kept waiting — and the full session fee still applies.

Technology trouble is its own category. If your video or connection fails, call or text right away and the session can often be finished by phone instead. A good-faith attempt to connect is never treated as a no-show.

The Grace Occurrence

Everyone gets one

The first late cancellation or no-show carries no charge, no matter the reason. Recovery and mental health work aren't tidy, and one missed session shouldn't come with a bill attached.

After that first occurrence, each subsequent late cancellation or no-show is billed at a flat fee of $100. This is a private-pay charge that insurance does not cover, and it's charged to the card kept securely on file.

When the Fee Is Waived

Real emergencies are always honored

Even beyond the grace occurrence, the fee is waived at clinical discretion for circumstances genuinely outside your control. That includes:

  • Sudden illness, injury, or a medical emergency — yours or a dependent's
  • A death in the family or a family crisis
  • Severe weather, evacuation orders, or a widespread power or internet outage
  • A mental health crisis that made attending impossible
  • Any situation where charging you would clearly do more harm than good

You will never be asked for documentation or proof. Simply reach out and explain the situation, and it will be handled with the benefit of the doubt.

Payment Method on File

How the fee is collected

A valid credit or debit card is kept securely on file through the practice's HIPAA-compliant billing system as a condition of scheduling. Late cancellation and no-show fees are charged to that card, and you'll always receive an emailed receipt. Card details are stored by the payment processor, not by the practice.

Repeated Missed Sessions

When it's worth a conversation

If missed sessions become a pattern — generally three or more within a short span — that's usually a signal worth exploring rather than a rule to enforce. Sometimes the appointment time no longer fits your life, sometimes ambivalence about treatment is showing up in the calendar, and sometimes something bigger is going on.

In those cases, the conversation comes first. If a schedule change or a different level of care would serve you better, that's discussed openly and a referral is offered. Standing appointment slots may be released after repeated no-shows so the time can be offered to someone on the waitlist, but the door stays open to return.

Cancellations From This End

It goes both ways

If a session has to be cancelled from this end — illness, emergency, or an unavoidable conflict — you'll be notified as early as possible and offered priority rescheduling, typically within the same week. You are never charged for a session cancelled by the practice.

Insurance Note

What insurers will and won't pay

Insurance plans, including Medicaid and Medicare, do not reimburse for missed appointments. Once insurance billing is active for your care, any late cancellation or no-show fee remains your direct responsibility and cannot be billed to your plan.

Questions

Ask before it's an issue

If anything here is unclear, or if your circumstances make a 24-hour window genuinely difficult to meet, say so during intake. Accommodations can often be worked out in advance — email admin@pridehealing.com or call (337) 283-9997.

Louisiana Medicaid

If Medicaid is your coverage, no fee applies

Louisiana Medicaid rules prohibit this practice from billing either Medicaid or you for an appointment that didn’t happen. That means no late-cancellation fee and no no-show fee is ever charged to a client covered by Louisiana Medicaid or a Medicaid managed care plan — there is no waiver or agreement that changes this, and you will never be asked to sign one.

The rest of this page still matters, though. Giving 24 hours’ notice when you can protects a session slot for someone else and keeps your own care on track. Missed sessions are still documented, and you may hear from the practice afterward to check in and get you rescheduled — that outreach is part of the care, not a penalty.

If a pattern of missed sessions continues, the practice may need to talk with you about whether this is the right fit right now. That conversation happens directly with you first, with referrals and help transitioning if care ends. Everyone else’s policy is identical apart from the fee itself, which state law removes.
